# This Spider Uses Trapped Fireflies to Lure in More Prey

**By Erin Garcia de Jesús**  
*Published on August 19, 2024*

## Introduction

Sometimes fireflies shouldn’t follow the light. Male fireflies, specifically *Abscondita terminalis*, typically use their bioluminescence to find females among tall grasses at dusk. However, when caught in the web of an orb weaver spider (*Araneus ventricosus*), these flashy males can become deadly decoys.

## Spider Behavior and Hunting Tactics

Researchers have reported that orb weaver spiders can manipulate male fireflies into flashing their lanterns in a female-like pattern, attracting more males into their webs. This extraordinary behavior allows spiders to gather an ample food supply.

### Research Findings

A team led by behavioral ecologist Daiqin Li from Hubei University in Wuhan, China, set up cameras to observe how fireflies behave when trapped in spider webs. The researchers conducted experiments by placing fireflies directly into the webs of orb weavers and either removing or leaving the spider present.

- **Observational Results:**  
  In the presence of *A. ventricosus* spiders, male fireflies were more likely to change their flashing patterns and become entangled in the webs. In contrast, they did not alter their behavior in the webs of other spider species.

- **Impact of Spider's Actions:**  
  When the spiders were around, the frequency of additional fireflies caught in the webs increased. Conversely, when the researchers painted over the fireflies’ flashing lanterns with black ink, the spiders captured fewer fireflies.

### Changes in Firefly Signals

Trapped male fireflies that were bitten by the spider ceased to use both of their lanterns and instead flashed just one, weakening their signals and making them appear more like females.

Li observed that the spiders treated fireflies differently than other non-flashing beetle species. Fireflies received a light wrapping in silk that left their lanterns visible, allowing the spiders to gather them in the web while waiting for more prey.

According to Li, as additional fireflies are caught, the spiders continue to repeat this process, compelling the fireflies to just keep flashing.

## Future Research Directions

The exact mechanism by which the fireflies' signals change remains unclear. Li suspects that the spider's bite or venom plays a significant role in altering these signals. He plans to investigate this further and will search for other orb weaver species outside of Southeast Asia that may employ similar tactics.
